# Amendment 5 — the structure of class B, its regulatory correspondence, and two evidence rules

Status: **additive and restrictive.** Nothing in the frozen taxonomy changes:
class A/B/C/D keep their definitions, every existing claim id keeps its
coding, and no reported figure moves. This amendment (i) records the
sub-structure that the first 21 verified units revealed inside class B,
(ii) fixes the regulatory instruments the study may and may not point at, and
(iii) adds two evidence rules that can only narrow what the study will accept.

---

## 1. Class B has three distinct forms, and they are not interchangeable

The frozen definition of class B — "the claimed licence genuinely exists but
the holder is not the onboarding entity" — turns out to cover three
mechanically different situations. The verified units separate cleanly:

| Form | What diverges | Verified instances |
|---|---|---|
| **B-scope** | The licence is real and held by a related entity, but its **authorised scope does not cover this reader** — wholesale-only permissions displayed on a retail-facing page; or an advice/introduction licence displayed on a trading site | An AFSL whose every authorisation row reads "Wholesale clients"; a Category-5 record limited to financial consultation and introduction |
| **B-domain** | The licence is real, but the **domain displaying it is not the domain the register publishes** for that holder | CySEC publishes an approved-domain field; in two verified units the observation domain appeared on neither the approved nor the non-approved list |
| **B-instrument** | The identifier displayed in a licence position is **a different kind of identifier** — a company-registry or global-business number, not a licence number | A Vanuatu company number; GB-prefixed Mauritius registration-category strings |

**Why this matters more than the aggregate count.** "Twelve of twenty-one
units are class B" is a weak sentence. "The divergence sits in scope, in
domain, and in identifier type — not in whether the licence exists" is the
finding, and it is the one the data actually supports: **of 21 verified
units, 20 resolved to a real register record.** The failure mode of this
ecosystem is not fabrication.

**Reporting requirement.** Every class-B count published by this study must
be reported with this three-way split. An undifferentiated class-B total may
not be presented as a headline.

## 2. Regulatory correspondence — what this study may point at

Recorded because the wrong instrument turns a verifiable observation into an
allegation of a criminal offence. The study describes **conduct that is
publicly observable**; it does not allege unlicensed operation.

**May be cited as the relevant regulatory frame:**

- **ASIC Act s12DA / s12DB and Corporations Act s1041H** — misleading or
  deceptive conduct in relation to financial services. This is the frame that
  fits a display which is literally accurate and collectively misleading.
- **ASIC RG 234** — accuracy and overall impression in the promotion of
  financial products.
- **FCA, *Our Contracts for Difference strategy* (13 December 2024)** —
  "halo firms": entities that exist to lend an FCA association to a wider
  group while the client contracts with an offshore entity. This is the
  regulator's own name for the pattern in class B.
- **CySEC Board Decision, 1 July 2024 (IC Markets (EU) Ltd, €200,000)** — an
  adjudicated finding that a CIF participated in activity resulting in the
  circumvention of leverage protections via a same-group third-country
  entity. Adjudicated facts, citable as such.
- **Wholesale/retail client thresholds** — the scope form (B-scope) is a
  cross-border instance of a domestic policy question about who counts as a
  wholesale client. Cited as context only; this study measures displays, not
  client classification.

**May NOT be cited:**

- **Corporations Act s911A (unlicensed financial services)** — this alleges a
  criminal offence. Nothing in this study's method can establish that any
  entity provided financial services without a licence: the study observes
  what a page displays and what a register records, and in 20 of 21 verified
  units the licence exists. Citing s911A would assert a conclusion the
  evidence cannot carry and would convert a measurement paper into an
  accusation.

This restriction binds all outputs, including abstracts, slides and
correspondence.

## 3. A voluntary regulator whitelist with two published gaps

ASIC announced (media release 26-122MR, 17 June 2026) that AFS licensees'
**website addresses** are being added to its professional registers:
collection opened 4 May 2026, publication from June 2026, with over 6,500
licensees invited to supply an address. Its stated purpose includes
supporting businesses — "including digital and social media platforms" — in
strengthening verification of financial-services advertising.

Two properties of that mechanism are on the record and are directly
measurable by this study's existing instruments:

1. **Supply is voluntary**, so coverage is unknown.
2. **Authorised representatives' websites are out of scope** by ASIC's own
   description.

Accordingly this study adds a secondary analysis, using the frame and
verification pipeline already frozen: for each verified unit whose displayed
authority is ASIC, record whether the observation domain appears in the
professional register's published website field, and report coverage and
non-coverage separately from the A/B/C/D coding.

**Framing, and why it is chosen.** This is an independent coverage audit of a
regulator's own new tool, published two months before this analysis. It is
constructive rather than accusatory, it states only facts of the form "this
domain does/does not appear in this register field", and it produces exactly
the feedback the mechanism's stated purpose calls for. It does not replace
the study's primary endpoint.

## 4. Two evidence rules (restrictive)

### 4.1 Future-dated sources are inadmissible

Any source is rejected outright, without further assessment, if it asserts an
event dated **later than the date of retrieval**.

This rule exists because a real instance was encountered: a site publishing a
"regulatory round-up" that described an enforcement action dated two weeks in
the future, in prose that was structurally complete, specific as to case
numbers, and professional in tone. Machine-generated pseudo-regulatory news
is now cheap enough that the register-verification discipline this study
applies to licence numbers must extend to **the regulatory record itself**.

### 4.2 Trade-press characterisations of regulatory documents are leads, not evidence

Any industry-media description of what a regulatory document says must be
checked against the document before it is used. A worked example: a trade
outlet described an ESMA statement as confirming that proprietary-trading
firms fall within CFD product-intervention measures; the statement's actual
subject is derivatives marketed as perpetual futures, and it does not
discuss proprietary-trading firms.

Trade press may be cited for what the industry **says about itself** — that
is primary evidence of marketing language, and is used as such. It may not be
cited for what a regulator has decided.

## 5. Two limits this study must state about its own evidence hierarchy

**Regulators make factual errors.** In *CFTC v. Traders Global Group Inc.*
the court dismissed the action with prejudice and sanctioned the regulator
under Rule 11, ordering it to bear costs exceeding US$3 million, after it
mischaracterised a tax payment as an asset transfer. This study treats
adjudicated regulatory findings as its strongest evidence tier; that tier has
a ceiling, and the ceiling is stated rather than assumed away.

**Sanction is not cessation.** Public reporting on the guarantee-market
ecosystem through 2025–2026 shows entities that were sanctioned, de-platformed
and prosecuted continuing under new names across multiple venues. No
inference of the form "this entity was sanctioned, therefore it is no longer
operating" may be drawn anywhere in this study.

---

## 6. Terminology

Where this study refers to hybrid investment-romance fraud, it does not use
the term "pig butchering" without qualification. That term has been
criticised in the peer-reviewed literature (Whittaker, Lazarus & Corcoran,
*Journal of Economic Criminology* 3:100052, 2024) for dehumanising victims.
First use notes the criticism; subsequent references use a neutral
description.
